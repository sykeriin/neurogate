NeuroGate

When an AI agent needs a human to approve a risky action, the moment it asks matters. NeuroGate reads the reviewer's workload from EEG and holds approval requests until they have the attention to judge them. Then it measures whether that catches more bad actions.

Why now

  • Approval gates are the default safety control for agents, and reviewers fatigue. "Oversight Has a Capacity" (arXiv 2606.08919) shows that escalating more can be less safe.
  • FIDES and CaMeL-style agents depend on human confirmation, which invites confirmation fatigue and social engineering.
  • EU AI Act Article 14 expects operators to stay aware of automation bias.
  • EEG-timed interruption already works for social messages (Kosmyna, Google). Nobody has tested it on security-relevant approvals with planted bad actions.

How it works

  1. Workload estimator. 8–10 minutes of 0/1/2-back calibration per user. It computes log(frontal-midline theta ÷ parietal alpha) on 2 s windows with 50% overlap, then z-scores it against the user's own baseline and smooths it with an EMA. It is relative, not absolute: cross-subject decoding on COG-BCI reaches only ~0.45 balanced accuracy.
  2. Risk-tiered queue. Tier 0 payments, deletes and external sends are never held past their deadline and need a reason to approve. Tier 1 requests are reversible and schedulable. Tier 2 requests are batched.
  3. Scheduler. It releases a request when load is in the user's own bottom 40% or at a subtask boundary, unless the deadline forces it. An anti-flood cap stops an attacker from draining attention by spamming approvals.
  4. Reviewer interface. It shows the diff (recipient, amount, path) and the content that triggered the action, and logs latency and load at the moment of each decision.

Immediateit arrives (today's default)

NeuroGateEEG load is low for this user, or at a boundary

24 participants, within-subject, Latin-square order, 75–90 min primary task. About 12% of requests are planted bad actions: lookalike recipients, inflated invoice amounts, injection-triggered exfiltration, and deletes aimed at protected folders.

First simulation: H2 is the real risk

The prototype's offline simulator runs every policy on the same seeded stream with 24 synthetic reviewers. Their detection falls with true workload and with bunched reviews. The EEG decoder is modelled as noisy (σ = 0.6, roughly AUC 0.7).

NeuroGate beats Immediate by +0.25 d′, with 95% CI [−0.15, 0.62], and the false-alarm rate stays flat. But it trails Boundary-only by −0.29 [−0.59, 0.01]. With a noisy decoder, releasing on "low load" sometimes fires mid-task, while boundaries are nearly always genuine low-load moments. Agent wait times: Immediate 0.5 s, NeuroGate 10 s, Boundary 31 s, Batch 143 s.

These are synthetic numbers from assumed reviewer and decoder models, not data. They mainly show the study design can detect differences of this size. They also suggest two changes before the pilot. First, gate NeuroGate's load release on decoder confidence. Second, test "boundary AND low load" as a sixth variant.

Hypotheses

  1. H1: NeuroGate gives a higher hit rate than Immediate, with no more false rejections.
  2. H2: NeuroGate beats Boundary-only. This is the primary confirmatory test, and it may fail.
  3. H3: Primary-task score and NASA-TLX are no worse than under Immediate.
  4. H4: Load at the moment of decision predicts missed bad actions (mixed-effects logistic model).

If H2 fails, the paper becomes "measured oversight capacity: what predicts missed bad approvals", which still yields the first public EEG + approval-decision dataset.

Ethics by design

  • Raw EEG never leaves the estimator process; the broker receives one scalar and a boundary flag.
  • No employer sees load data. A visible switch turns the load signal off at any time.
  • The misuse case, pushing approvals at tired moments, is named in the paper, and the release enforces the opposite policy.
  • Institutional ethics approval and informed consent come before any recording. Neural data is regulated in CA, CO, CT, MT and MN.
